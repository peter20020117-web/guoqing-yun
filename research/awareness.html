Awareness

Portobello Beach

The second work marks the turning point of the project, where restoration begins to emerge. At Portobello Beach, I gradually became aware of the simple, repetitive sounds of waves, wind and seabirds. The openness of the environment created a sense of dynamic tranquillity, and repeated visits allowed me to recognise how shifting my attention could interrupt the grip of anxiety and lead toward greater control of my inner state. This process of noticing the environment and recognising my own changing attention is the basis of “Awareness.”

To create a contrast with Cacophony while documenting this gradual process of self-discovery, I chose a small chamber ensemble. The piano represents the waves, while the strings express inner changes and support the emotional development. The piano uses a continuously repeating ascending motif based on an eight-bar loop; the same motif later returns with different chord progressions. The first half is centred on C, while at around three minutes the music modulates to F and becomes more solemn and weighty. At 4:40, the emotional climax is reached: the piano texture becomes denser as the left hand changes from an ascending two-note pattern to an ascending three-note pattern, allowing the lead to pass to the cello.

The string section enters progressively from the upper to lower registers as harmonic support. Around the four-minute point, the solo cello carries the main melody while two groups of violins and violas sustain notes in four-measure units. The cello uses ascending scales in one-beat units grouped into four beats; later, the viola takes over inner-voice movement while the cello increasingly assumes the melodic role. The seaside soundscape is built from two layers: distant ambient recordings dominated by wind and seabirds, and a large collection of wave recordings. I cut continuous wave recordings into individual waves and edited them to follow the piano rhythm, while arranging them from gentle to intense so that the environmental sound itself participates in the build toward the climax.

For the Dolby Atmos mix, the three ambient tracks were organised according to their spatial qualities. Two layers of ocean waves were placed primarily below the listening position, creating an open and stable underlying layer, while another ambient layer was positioned above and behind. The piano was placed toward the upper rear of the sound field, separated spatially from the chordal material. The strings were arranged from front to back according to register, producing depth across the soundstage and surrounding the listening position. Wide “Size” values were applied to the ambient sounds to extend their spatial range and strengthen the immersive character of the seaside environment.
